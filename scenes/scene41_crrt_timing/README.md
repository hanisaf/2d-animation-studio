## Clinical notes

The answer is **C**. The patient has KDIGO stage 3 AKI but none of the urgent indications represented here: refractory hyperkalemia, severe/refractory metabolic acidosis, pulmonary edema causing hypoxemia, or uremic complications. Close reassessment is essential; watchful waiting is not indefinite.

STARRT-AKI randomized 3,019 patients and found no 90-day mortality benefit with accelerated initiation (approximately 44% in each arm). Accelerated initiation increased adverse events and 90-day RRT dependence (10.4% versus 6.0%); approximately 38% assigned to the standard strategy never received RRT. AKIKI and IDEAL-ICU likewise found no survival advantage to early initiation, and a 2020 individual-patient-data meta-analysis reached the same broad conclusion.

AKIKI-2 addressed a later boundary: after oliguria persisted beyond 72 hours or BUN exceeded 112 mg/dL, postponing still further until BUN exceeded 140 mg/dL or an urgent indication arose offered no benefit and may have caused harm. The threshold in choice D is therefore not a safe stand-alone rule.

The modality note in choice B is separate from the timing decision. In a hemodynamically unstable patient receiving vasopressors, CRRT would generally be preferred if an indication develops. The supplied question also cites the 2026 ICRAKI trial; this animation preserves that teaching point in the documentation while keeping the on-screen explanation focused on the established timing decision.
