## Clinical notes

The correct answer is **B**. As supplied in the question, ICRAKI was a 2026 multicenter noninferiority randomized trial of 554 critically ill adults. It was stopped early after iHD failed to meet noninferiority for major adverse kidney events by day 90: 78.1% with iHD versus 71.6% with CRRT. Ninety-day mortality was 63.1% versus 53.5% (hazard ratio 1.30; 95% CI 1.04–1.63).

This finding should be understood against older trials and meta-analyses, which generally found no mortality or renal-recovery advantage between initial iHD and CRRT. The classic Hemodiafe trial reported 60-day survival of 32% versus 33% (P=0.98), not superiority of iHD. “Equivalent for every outcome” nevertheless overstates those older data.

CRRT is generally preferred rather than contraindicated when marked hemodynamic instability makes rapid fluid and solute shifts undesirable. In acute brain injury or fulminant hepatic failure, iHD may create faster osmotic shifts and a greater disequilibrium/cerebral-edema risk, so CRRT is commonly favored.
